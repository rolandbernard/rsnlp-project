This appendix section contains additional dialogue examples generated by various models during the experiment evaluation. The focus is mainly on showing examples for the commonly seen mistakes identified in \cref{qualitative}. Note that like in \cref{fig:example_dialogue} the initial prompt and repetition of the other players messages have been shortened for brevity, indicated by \textit{[...]}. The complete initial prompt template for the cooperative game mode is as follows.

\begin{quote}
\small
\begin{verbatim}
You are playing a cooperative negotiation game in which you have to agree on how to divide a set
 of items among you and another player.

Rules:
(a) You and the other player are given a set of items. Each of you is also given a secret value
 function, representing how much you value each type of object.
(b) You exchange messages with the other player to agree on who gets which items. You can send a
 maximum of $N$ messages each, or terminate early by making a secret proposal at any time.
(c) You are each asked to submit a secret proposal indicating the items you want formatted in
 square brackets as follows: "[Proposal: <number> <object name>, <number> <object name>, <...>]"
(d) If your proposals are complementary, i.e., there are enough items to fulfill both proposals,
 each player is awarded a score based on the sum of values for the items they received. Otherwise,
 both of you get zero points.
(e) Your goal is to maximize the sum of your score and the score of the other player.

Let us start.

The set of available items is:

$ITEMS$

Your secret value function is:

$VALUE_FUNCTION$

IMPORTANT: Your messages, unless it is the secret proposal, are directly transmitted to the other
 player, so do not include any response to the rules or text announcing your message. To make a
 secret proposal, use the indicated format. Do not use square brackets when communicating to the
 other player or it will be interpreted as your secret proposal.
\end{verbatim}
\end{quote}

Note that \inlinecode{\$N\$}, \inlinecode{\$ITEMS\$}, and \inlinecode{\$VALUE_FUNCTION\$} will be replaced based on the items and maximum turns in the game instance, and for the specific value function of the player. Each player will therefore have a slightly different initial prompt. The initial prompts for the semi-competitive and strictly competitive game settings differ only in that they do not mention the game being cooperative, and in that the last rule, rule (e), indicates a different optimization objective. The prompts for the other languages contain the same instructions, only having been translated into either Italian or German.

There are two additional prompt templates, one used for when one of the players has already made a secret proposal. In this case the other player is not given the proposal, but instead prompted with the following.

\begin{quote}
\small
\begin{verbatim}
The other player has submitted a secret proposal. It is now your turn to submit a proposal of your own.
\end{verbatim}
\end{quote}

Finally, there is also a prompt template for when the maximum number of turns has been reached. In this case, the game master still transmits to the other player the last message, and immediately follows it with the following message instructing the player to make a proposal.

\begin{quote}
\small
\begin{verbatim}
The time is up. It is now your turn to submit a secret proposal.
\end{verbatim}
\end{quote}
